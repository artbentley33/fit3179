Australian Specialty Cheese

An exploration of Australia's cheese industry

Introduction

Australia's cheese industry is part of a wider dairy sector connecting farming, food production, domestic consumption, and international trade. From dairy farms supplying raw milk to producers creating different cheese varieties, each stage supports local businesses and supplies products for domestic consumers and overseas markets.

This visualisation explores five aspects of the industry, including milk production, cheese characteristics, consumption, international trade, and industry recognition. Together, these perspectives reveal production patterns, consumer preferences, key trading partners, and the strengths of Australia's cheese industry.

Conclusion

Australia's cheese industry combines large-scale production with a diverse range of products. Cheddar leads production, while cottage cheese has relatively lower fat and SNF content in the dataset. Consumption trends also highlight the role of cheese in Australian diets and the connection between production and domestic demand.

International trade connects Australian cheese to overseas markets, with Japan a leading export destination and New Zealand a major import source. Competition results provide another measure of industry performance, with Real Dairy Australia leading by medals and NSW and Victoria receiving strong recognition.
